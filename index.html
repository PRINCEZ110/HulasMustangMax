THE MUSTANG MAX SERIES.
NEPAL'S JEEP OF 1998–2013.

Introduction

Developed from necessity, the Hulas Mustang of 1998 was the first of its kind in Nepal. With dozer-track fame across hills and plains it became the most iconic made-in-Nepal workhorse.

Production Models

Engineers under chief engineer Prafulla Das built the future Mustang from the best usable parts for Nepali roads — planning 150 jeeps a year.

When the VVIP jeep reached Kathmandu, it was a sensation. A made-in-Nepal jeep carrying the prime minister was a new benchmark.

National History

Like the road jeep, the workshop run was an outstanding effort — from Golcha Group's 1998 start to the PM's garage — until the 2012 Euro-3 budget rule ended it.

What people said
at the time.

The Mustang isn't about transportation, it's about reaching. Hard. Loaded. Every day. A hill-legal workhorse more at home on a dozer track than a boulevard.

Prafulla Das, former GM & chief engineer, Hulas Motors

Summary: “Let's not reopen old wounds… As a noble citizen, I wanted to do something for the country.” Two decades of work, two months to build the AC VVIP Mustang Max for PM Baburam Bhattarai (Ba 1 Jha 9360).

Hulas Bravo (Rs 2–3M, 11 seats) took Rs 8M+ R&D to fight Rs 6–10M imports. The 2012 budget allowed only Euro-3 registrations — team asked for 2 years, got 1. Production stopped 2013. Bravo never reached the road.
